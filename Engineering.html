The Evolution

Two Cars, One Relentless Redesign

Every Aerostorm car carries a version number that tracks its design iteration count — 2.27, for example, marks the 27th iteration of our second concept line. It's a small detail, but an honest one: nothing on the car arrived on the first try.

AR Nimbus 4.51 didn't start on a clean sheet — it's a direct rebuild of what we learned racing AR Ventus 2.27 at regionals.

Regional Qualifier

AR Ventus 2.27

  • —A gap between the wheel and sidepod edge increased friction and hurt aerodynamics.
  • —Ran a plain axle-hole wheel system with no bearings, costing performance.
Nationals · Current

AR Nimbus 4.51

  • —Sidepod lengthened within regs while managing weight — airflow is more linear and the part is actually manufacturable on the CNC.
  • —Replaced the axle-hole system with Axle → Bearing Holder → Wheel, using F1 Bearing's Hybrid Ceramic Bearings.
Airflow First

Designed to Cut Through the Air

Tapered, pointed nose minimizes the stagnation zone, while the body's top surface transitions gradually into the cockpit hump with no sharp corners — keeping airflow attached longer and avoiding early boundary-layer separation and turbulent wake.

Frontal cross-sectional area minimized (bluff-body minimization) to cut drag.

Rear wing tilted 10° — a deliberately moderate angle, enough for downforce and grip without excessive drag on a straight track.

Validated in Airshaper, a browser-based CFD/CAA tool, to visualize airflow — this is what caught the Ventus sidepod flaw before Nimbus was built.

The Numbers Behind It

Physics, Not Guesswork

F = ma

Used to compare material acceleration — PLA-CF's lower mass versus alternatives gave better acceleration.

Fd = ½ρCdAv2

The drag equation, used to reason about reducing frontal area and drag.

~15 m/s target

Early-stage balloon-rocket experiments estimated achievable velocity, targeting roughly an 80% drag reduction from initial concepts.

From CAD to Car

Built By Hand, Machined With Precision

Prototyping — test iterations 3D-printed on a Bambu Lab A1, reusing old and near-empty filament spools and parts from prior prototypes.

Manufacturing — CNC-machined by Stemplify, our manufacturing partner.

Hand-finishing — axle holes drilled at home, front wing joints sawed for proper fit, and loose-fitting pieces hand-assembled with super glue.

Paint — sprayed by sponsor House of Polish.

Reality Check

What the Track Taught Us

Bearing Fix, On the Fly

Testing at the Official STEM Racing India track surfaced a bearing-rotation issue that was loosening the wheel. Fixed by adding a thin layer of paint over the axle protrusion that inserts into the bearing, tightening the joint.

Simulated vs. Real Weight

Fusion's simulated weight estimates were well off from the real build: the bearing assembly alone came in at ~34g, heavier than expected, and the real main body weighed 68g against Fusion's simulated 36g — roughly double. A lesson learned for the next iteration, not a failure.
